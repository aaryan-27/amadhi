For an Indian office, plan on **70–90 sq ft per person** of carpet area, including meeting rooms, pantry and circulation. Dense open-plan layouts get to 60; a design-led office with generous meeting space runs past 110.

That gives roughly:

| Team | Carpet area to plan for |
|---|---|
| 10 people | 700 – 900 sq ft |
| 25 people | 1,750 – 2,250 sq ft |
| 50 people | 3,500 – 4,500 sq ft |
| 100 people | 7,000 – 9,000 sq ft |

## The trap: which "area" are you being quoted?

This is where numbers stop matching. Three terms, routinely used loosely:

- **Carpet area** — the usable floor inside your walls. What your people actually occupy.
- **Built-up area** — carpet plus walls and internal structure.
- **Super built-up area** — built-up plus your share of lobbies, lifts, corridors and services.

Indian commercial leases are very often quoted on **super built-up**, and the gap is large: a loading factor of 25–40% is normal. A "5,000 sq ft" office quoted super built-up may be around 3,500 sq ft of carpet — enough for roughly 40 people, not 60.

**Always ask which basis the quote uses, and what the loading factor is.** Then do your per-person maths on carpet area only.

## What drives the number up or down

- **Meeting rooms.** The biggest single variable. A team doing constant client calls needs several rooms and call booths; a quiet team needs one.
- **Layout.** Benching is denser than fixed desks. Cabins for senior staff consume area quickly.
- **Amenities.** A real pantry, a wellness room or a reception all take space that doesn't seat anyone.
- **Hybrid attendance.** If only 60% of the team is in on a given day, you can size for peak attendance rather than headcount — but size for the *peak*, not the average, or Tuesdays don't work.
- **Growth.** Sizing for today means moving in eighteen months. Most teams add 10–20% headroom.

## When this matters — and when it doesn't

In **coworking or a managed office you buy seats, not area**. The operator has already solved density, and the sq ft figure is mostly irrelevant to your bill.

It matters when you are **comparing a conventional lease against flexible workspace**, because that is the only way to put them on the same footing: take the lease's rent per sq ft, multiply by the area you actually need, add fit-out, maintenance, utilities and staff, then divide by headcount to get a real per-seat cost. Compared like that, flexible workspace usually wins below a certain size and a certain time horizon.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many square feet per person is standard for an office in India?

Plan 70–90 sq ft of carpet area per person including meeting rooms, pantry and circulation. Dense layouts reach 60; generous ones exceed 110.

### How much office space does a team of 25 need?

Roughly 1,750–2,250 sq ft of carpet area. If the quote is on super built-up area, the equivalent figure will be significantly higher — commonly 25–40% more.

### What is the difference between carpet area and super built-up area?

Carpet area is the usable space inside your walls. Super built-up adds your share of common areas such as lobbies, lifts and corridors. Indian leases are often quoted on super built-up, so always confirm the basis and the loading factor before comparing.

### Does sq ft per person matter for coworking?

Not really. In coworking and managed offices you pay per seat and the operator has already fixed the density. The figure matters mainly when comparing a conventional lease against flexible workspace on equal terms.

### Should I size my office for headcount or attendance?

For peak attendance if you work hybrid, plus headroom for growth. Sizing for average attendance leaves the busiest days short of desks.

### How much extra space should I allow for growth?

Most teams add 10–20% beyond current need. Beyond that you pay for empty desks; below it you risk moving again within a couple of years.
