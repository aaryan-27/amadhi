Coworking sells you **seats on a shared floor**. A managed office gives you a **private, branded floor that someone else builds and operates** for a single monthly fee. Both are flexible and furnished; the real difference is whether you share the space and how long you commit.

For most teams the deciding factor is not the headline rate. It is how confident you are about headcount twelve months from now.

## What you are actually buying

**Coworking** is priced per desk. You get a workstation on a shared floor, with meeting rooms, pantry, internet and housekeeping run by the operator and shared with other companies. You can usually add or drop a few seats with a month's notice.

**A managed office** is priced per seat too, but you take the whole suite. The operator fits it out to your layout, puts your name on the door, runs the facilities, and bills one amount covering rent, maintenance, utilities and services. Terms typically run one to three years.

The industry sometimes calls managed offices "office-as-a-service" or "built-to-suit flex". They are the same thing.

## Where the costs actually differ

Per seat, coworking usually looks cheaper at small headcounts and the gap narrows as you grow, because a managed office spreads its fixed costs — reception, pantry, meeting rooms — across more of your own people instead of across other companies.

What that comparison hides is everything outside the desk rate:

- **Meeting rooms.** In coworking these are shared and usually capped, with extra hours billed. In a managed office they are yours.
- **Deposit.** Both take one, commonly two to three months. A longer managed-office term sometimes means a larger one.
- **Fit-out.** In a managed office the operator funds it and recovers it through the monthly fee, which is why the term is longer.
- **Growth.** Adding four seats to a coworking plan is routine. Adding four desks to a managed suite means the suite had to be sized for them.

## Choose coworking when

- Headcount might change by more than 20% in a year
- You are under roughly 20 people
- You want to be in next week, not next quarter
- Client visits are occasional rather than constant
- You would rather not sign a multi-year commitment

## Choose a managed office when

- You need a private, badged floor for compliance, client confidentiality or data rules
- Headcount is steady and known
- Your brand needs to be visible to people who visit
- You want one invoice instead of a rent bill plus service charges plus utilities
- You are above roughly 30 people, where the per-seat gap narrows

## The honest middle

Plenty of teams do both: a managed suite for the core team and coworking seats in another city for a small sales pod. Nothing stops you, and operators are used to it.

If you are genuinely unsure, take coworking. It is the reversible decision. A managed office signed at the wrong size is expensive to unwind; a coworking plan that turns out too small is a phone call.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a managed office cheaper than coworking?

Not usually at small headcounts. Per seat, coworking tends to be cheaper below roughly 20–30 people, and the two converge as team size grows because a managed office spreads its fixed costs across your own headcount rather than across other companies.

### What is the difference between a coworking space and a managed office?

Coworking is shared: you take desks on a floor used by several companies. A managed office is private: you take the whole suite, fitted out for you and operated by the provider, with your branding and your own access control.

### What is the minimum term for a managed office in India?

Commonly one to three years, because the operator is funding the fit-out and recovering it over the term. Coworking plans are usually month-to-month or annual.

### Can I move from coworking into a managed office with the same operator?

Often yes, and it is worth asking before you sign anything. Operators with both products will usually let a growing team transfer, and some will credit part of your coworking spend towards the move.

### Do I need to furnish a managed office myself?

No. The operator delivers it furnished, cabled and serviced. That is what separates it from a conventional lease, where the fit-out is your project and your capital.
