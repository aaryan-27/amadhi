Almost every coworking negotiation focuses on the desk rate. The clauses that decide what you actually pay over two years are further down the document, and they are usually the ones nobody reads: **escalation, lock-in, notice, deposit refund, and the definition of "included"**.

None of this is unusual or predatory. It is simply where the money is.

> General information, not legal advice. Have a lawyer review anything you sign.

## The six that matter

### 1. Lock-in versus term

These are different things and get confused constantly. The **term** is how long the agreement runs. The **lock-in** is the period you cannot exit without paying anyway. A one-year term with a six-month lock-in means you can leave after six months with notice; a one-year lock-in means you pay for twelve regardless.

Ask for the lock-in in months, in writing, and check it matches your own certainty about headcount.

### 2. Notice period

Commonly one to three months. What matters is when notice can be *given* — some agreements only allow it in a window before renewal, which quietly turns a one-month notice into a much longer commitment.

### 3. Escalation

Annual increases of a few per cent are standard. Check whether escalation applies to the desk rate only or to the whole invoice including services, and whether it compounds. Over a three-year managed-office term this is the single biggest number in the document after the rate itself.

### 4. What "included" means

Get the list in writing, with quantities:

- Meeting-room hours per seat per month, and the rate beyond them
- Printing allowance
- Parking — how many bays, and at what cost
- Internet: shared bandwidth or a dedicated line, and who fixes it
- Power backup and air conditioning **outside** business hours, which is where "24x7 access" often quietly stops
- Housekeeping frequency
- Guest and visitor policy

Two quotes at the same headline price routinely differ by thousands a month once this list is compared properly.

### 5. Deposit and its refund

Two to three months is normal. The clause to read is the **refund**: how many days after exit, what can be deducted, and who decides the condition of the space. Ask for a timeline in days and a joint inspection at handover.

### 6. Exit condition

What state must you leave the space in, and who pays for restoring anything you changed? If you put up signage, ran extra cabling or repainted, this clause decides the bill.

## Smaller clauses worth a look

- **Transfer and assignment** — can you move seats to a group company or a new entity after a restructure?
- **Downsizing** — can you drop seats mid-term, and with what notice?
- **Relocation rights** — can the operator move you to another floor or building, and on what terms?
- **Service interruption** — what happens if internet or power fails for a day, and is there any credit?
- **Termination for default** — how many days to remedy a late payment before they can act?

## What to ask for

You have most leverage before you sign and almost none afterwards. Reasonable asks:

- A shorter lock-in in exchange for a slightly higher rate
- Escalation capped, and applied to desk rate only
- Deposit refund within a stated number of days
- Meeting-room hours that roll over, or a higher monthly allowance
- A fixed rate for additional seats, so growth doesn't get repriced

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between lock-in and notice period in a coworking agreement?

Lock-in is the minimum period you must pay for even if you leave. Notice period is how far ahead you must tell the operator you are going. A six-month lock-in with one month's notice means you can give notice in month five and leave at the end of month six.

### What is a normal security deposit for coworking in India?

Commonly two to three months of the monthly fee, refundable at exit subject to deductions. The refund timeline and what can be deducted should be stated in the agreement.

### Is the rate in a coworking agreement negotiable?

Usually, particularly on longer terms, larger seat counts or quieter buildings. Terms such as lock-in length, escalation and included meeting-room hours are often easier to move than the headline rate.

### What does "all inclusive" actually cover?

It varies by operator, so insist on an itemised list. Typically it covers the desk, internet, electricity, housekeeping and pantry basics. Meeting-room hours beyond an allowance, parking, printing and after-hours air conditioning are the items most often billed separately.

### Can I reduce seats during the term?

Only if the agreement allows it. Many permit a reduction with notice after the lock-in; some do not permit it at all. If your headcount is uncertain, negotiate this clause specifically rather than assuming flexibility.

### Do I pay brokerage on a coworking agreement?

Not through Amadhi — it is free for occupiers, and you pay the operator directly at their own rate. If another intermediary is involved, ask who pays them and how much before you sign.
