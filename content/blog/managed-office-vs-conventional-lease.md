A conventional lease is quoted in **rent per square foot**. A managed office is quoted in **one monthly fee per seat**. Compared as printed, the lease almost always looks cheaper — and usually isn't, because the per-sq-ft figure is a fraction of what the office ends up costing.

To compare them honestly you have to add everything the lease number leaves out.

## What the lease rate excludes

- **Fit-out** — furniture, cabling, partitions, HVAC modifications, electricals. A capital cost you fund up front, often the largest single number in the project.
- **CAM** (common area maintenance) — billed per sq ft on top of rent, monthly.
- **Utilities** — electricity, water, diesel for backup, all metered separately.
- **Internet and IT** — your contract, your problem when it fails.
- **Housekeeping, security, pantry, reception** — your staff or your vendors.
- **Property tax and insurance**, depending on how the lease is structured.
- **Management time** — someone in your team runs the building relationship. That person has a salary.
- **Deposit** — commonly six months or more, versus two to three for flexible workspace.
- **Dilapidations** — restoring the space at exit.

A managed office folds nearly all of that into the monthly fee. That is what you are paying the premium for.

## The comparison that works

Put both on a **cost per seat per month** basis over the period you'll actually stay:

1. Take the lease rent per sq ft, multiplied by the area you truly need — on **carpet** area, not super built-up ([how to size it](/blog/office-leasing/sq-ft-per-person-office)).
2. Add CAM, utilities, internet, housekeeping and security, monthly.
3. Amortise the fit-out and the deposit's opportunity cost across the lease term.
4. Add the salary share of whoever manages it.
5. Divide by headcount.

Run the same exercise for a three-year horizon and a five-year one. The answer often flips: the lease's fit-out is spread thinner the longer you stay, which is precisely why leases reward certainty and punish change.

## Where each genuinely wins

**A managed office wins when:**

- You need space in weeks, not quarters
- Headcount may change
- You don't want capital sunk into furniture
- You have no facilities team and no wish to build one
- The horizon is under about three years

**A conventional lease wins when:**

- You are confident about five-plus years and stable headcount
- You want a space designed exactly to your specification
- You are large enough that per-seat economics turn in your favour
- You want the asset relationship — naming rights, signage, long-term control
- You have, or will have, a facilities function anyway

## The part teams underestimate

Not money: **time**. A lease means finding space, negotiating heads of terms, legal review, design, contractor tendering, fit-out, inspections and statutory approvals. Six months is normal, and it lands on people who also have day jobs.

A managed office is a signature and a move-in date. For a team that needs to be operational this quarter, that difference often decides it regardless of cost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a managed office more expensive than a conventional lease?

Per square foot, yes. Per seat over a realistic holding period, often not — once fit-out, CAM, utilities, internet, housekeeping, security, deposit and management time are added to the lease rate. Compare cost per seat per month over the period you will actually stay.

### What is included in a managed office fee?

Typically rent, fit-out, maintenance, utilities, internet, housekeeping, security and reception in one monthly amount per seat. Confirm the itemised list, as inclusions vary by operator.

### How long is a typical managed office term?

Usually one to three years, because the operator funds the fit-out and recovers it over the term. Conventional leases in India commonly run three, five or nine years with agreed lock-ins.

### What deposit does each require?

Flexible workspace is commonly two to three months. Conventional leases often ask six months or more, which is real working capital sitting idle for the term.

### How long does each take to occupy?

A managed office can be occupied within days or weeks of signing since it is delivered furnished and connected. A lease plus fit-out commonly takes several months from heads of terms to move-in.

### When does a conventional lease make more sense?

When headcount is stable, the horizon is five years or more, you want a bespoke design, and you already have or want a facilities capability. The longer you stay, the more the up-front fit-out is spread and the better the lease looks.
