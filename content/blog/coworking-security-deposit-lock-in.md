A coworking security deposit in India is commonly **two to three months of the monthly fee**, refundable when you leave. Lock-in — the period you pay for whether or not you stay — commonly runs **three to twelve months**, and longer for managed offices where the operator funded a fit-out.

Those ranges are not where teams lose money. The losses come from the refund clause and from misunderstanding what lock-in means.

> General information, not legal advice. Have anything you sign reviewed.

## What the deposit is for

It covers unpaid invoices, damage beyond fair wear and tear, and the cost of restoring anything you changed. It is not a fee, and it is not the operator's money — it should come back.

Larger deposits are usually asked for when the entity is newly incorporated, foreign, or has no credit history in India. That is negotiable, particularly if you can offer a longer term or pay a quarter up front.

## The refund clause is the one to read

Ask for three things in writing:

1. **A timeline in days.** "Within 30 days of exit" is specific. "In due course" is not.
2. **What can be deducted**, itemised, and who decides the condition of the space.
3. **A joint inspection at handover**, with a signed note of the condition.

Without a joint inspection you are arguing about the state of a desk weeks after you handed back the keys, with no record. That is the single most common reason deposits come back short.

## Lock-in, plainly

**Lock-in** is the minimum you pay for. **Notice** is how far ahead you must say you are leaving. **Term** is how long the agreement runs. Three different things, routinely conflated.

A twelve-month term with a six-month lock-in and one month's notice means: you can give notice in month five and walk away at the end of month six, paying for six months in total.

Breaking a lock-in usually means paying the remaining months, though operators will often negotiate if they can re-let the seats — especially in a building with a waiting list. Ask; the worst answer is no.

## What is genuinely negotiable

- **Deposit size**, especially in exchange for a longer term or advance payment
- **Refund timeline** — ask for a stated number of days
- **Lock-in length**, often traded against the monthly rate
- **A downsizing right** after the lock-in, which matters more than most teams realise
- **Escalation**, which is easier to cap than the headline rate is to cut

The time to ask is before signing. Afterwards you have no leverage at all.

## Before you hand back the space

- Give notice in the format the agreement requires — email to a named person, usually
- Settle invoices; unpaid amounts come straight out of the deposit
- Remove signage, cabling and anything you installed
- Do the joint inspection and get it signed
- Confirm the refund date in writing

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is a security deposit for a coworking space in India?

Commonly two to three months of the monthly fee. Newly incorporated or foreign entities are sometimes asked for more, which is usually negotiable against a longer term or advance payment.

### Is a coworking security deposit refundable?

Yes, subject to deductions for unpaid invoices, damage beyond fair wear and tear, and restoring changes you made. Get the refund timeline in days and do a joint inspection at handover so the condition is recorded.

### What is a typical lock-in period for coworking?

Three to twelve months for coworking. Managed offices run longer, often one to three years, because the operator funded the fit-out and recovers it across the term.

### What happens if I leave before the lock-in ends?

You normally remain liable for the remaining lock-in period. Many operators will negotiate if they can re-let the seats, so it is worth asking rather than assuming the full amount is fixed.

### Can I negotiate the deposit or the lock-in?

Often, particularly on longer terms or larger seat counts. Operators tend to move more readily on deposit size, refund timeline and escalation than on the headline desk rate.

### When should I get my deposit back?

Whenever the agreement says — which is why it should state a number of days. Thirty to sixty days after exit is common. Settle outstanding invoices and complete the joint inspection first, as both are usual preconditions.
