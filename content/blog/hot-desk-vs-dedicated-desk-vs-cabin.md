Coworking sells the same floor three ways. A **hot desk** is any free seat, first come first served. A **dedicated desk** is a specific seat that stays yours. A **private cabin** is a lockable room for your team only.

Price rises in that order. The useful question is not which is best but a practical one: **does anyone on your team need to leave equipment, papers or a second monitor on the desk overnight?** If yes, a hot desk will frustrate them within a fortnight, whatever it saves.

## Hot desk

You turn up and take whatever seat is free, clearing it when you leave.

**Works for:** solo founders, consultants in the office two or three days a week, sales teams who are mostly out, anyone testing a new city before committing.

**Doesn't work for:** anyone with a desktop, dual monitors, drawing equipment or physical files. Nor for teams who want to sit together reliably — on a busy morning you may not.

Watch for: whether your plan caps days per month, and whether there is anywhere to store things between visits.

## Dedicated desk

A specific desk with your name on it. Leave your monitor and drawers set up; come in whenever you like.

**Works for:** small teams who want to sit together, anyone in five days a week, people with equipment.

**Doesn't work for:** teams needing confidential calls all day, or anyone with client data that shouldn't be visible on an open floor.

Watch for: whether the team's desks are actually adjacent — not guaranteed unless you ask — and whether a locker or pedestal is included.

## Private cabin

A lockable room, usually priced per seat with a minimum. Your whiteboard, your door, your calls.

**Works for:** teams of four and up, anyone handling sensitive data, teams whose day is mostly calls, and anyone needing a room that looks like theirs when a client visits.

**Doesn't work for:** a team of two paying a four-seat minimum, or anyone who values the buzz of an open floor.

Watch for: the seat minimum, whether the price includes the room or is per occupied seat, and whether you can add a seat mid-term without repricing the room.

## How the prices compare

The gap between the three is usually larger than the gap between two buildings offering the same product. A cabin can cost several times a hot desk in the same space, because you are buying floor area and privacy rather than a chair.

## A quick way to decide

1. Does anyone need to leave things on the desk overnight? → **Dedicated desk** at minimum.
2. Are more than half your working hours on calls, or do you handle confidential data? → **Cabin**.
3. Is the team four or more and likely to grow? → **Cabin**, and check the cost of adding a seat.
4. None of the above, and you are in fewer than three days a week? → **Hot desk**.

You can also mix: a cabin for the team that is always in, plus two hot desks for people who drop in. Most operators allow it on one agreement.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a hot desk and a dedicated desk?

A hot desk is any available seat, taken first come first served and cleared when you leave. A dedicated desk is a specific seat reserved for you permanently, so you can leave equipment set up and come and go as you like.

### Is a private cabin worth the extra cost?

It is when your work needs privacy — constant calls, confidential client data, or client visits where the room should look like yours. For a small team that is mostly quiet and mostly out, a dedicated desk usually delivers the same value for less.

### How much more does a dedicated desk cost than a hot desk?

Consistently more, because the seat is held for you whether or not you use it, but the gap varies by operator and building. Our city price guides show the current spread by micro-market.

### Can I switch between them later?

Usually, subject to availability and notice. Moving up — hot desk to dedicated, dedicated to cabin — is normally straightforward. Moving down may be restricted until the lock-in ends, so check that clause before you sign.

### Do private cabins have a minimum number of seats?

Most do, commonly around three or four. A two-person team may still pay the minimum, which is often what makes a dedicated desk the better value at that size.

### Which is best for a team of five?

Usually a cabin, since five people typically clear the seat minimum and gain a room for calls and client meetings. Dedicated desks remain sensible if the five rarely overlap in the office on the same days.
